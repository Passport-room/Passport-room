About the CEO and the team behind Passport Room

Passport Room is built by a small team in Jamalpur, Bangladesh — not by a photo lab, a visa agency or an investor-backed startup. This page is about the founder, the people who work with him, and the reason a passport photo tool ended up being free.

A message from the founder

Hey! I am the CEO and founder of Passport Room. About me: I am a young boy from Bangladesh. I have learned coding, video editing and mastered AI and pro conversation. I can learn new things fast. I love natural and peaceful places. My Goal: On the internet there are several tools for making passport photos. But none of these are perfect. If one is free it has not all necessary features or good UI, again if a tool is good, it may be paid or have photo leak problems. So I, with my team, built a perfect free tool containing all necessary features like background removal, Dress changer, AI enhancer, Custom adjustment, Custom size (roughly supporting all country sizes on earth), BG colour, A4 sheet and more sizes, sheet editor, Single download, Directly print option, all there are free without any watermark in browser. We may earn from some ads (we specifically designed them so the ads never disturb you) with our partner Google AdSense. Our team is working to make it better day by day. If you have a suggestion please inform us on support.passport.room@gmail.com This is a part of the mega project OneRoom. Thank you for being with us.

Written by the founder in his own words, with only spelling and grammar corrected.

Who you are actually talking to

There is no call centre behind the support address. Passport Room is run by a founder who writes most of the product decisions himself, a couple of developers who handle the browser side of the app, and the people who check that each country preset matches what the authority actually publishes. Everybody is based in Bangladesh, and everybody works on the same tool you are using right now.

That is also why answers can be slow some weeks and quick on others. If you send a bug report, it goes to the founder first, not into a ticket queue that nobody reads.

How the tool is put together

The photo you pick is read inside the browser and stays there. Background removal, the face crop, the colour work, the outfit try-on and the print sheet all run on your own device, so there is no upload step and no server holding a picture of your face. The model files download once and are cached, which is why the tool still works after you go offline.

Size presets come from what each authority publishes — 51 × 51 mm for the United States, 35 × 45 mm for the United Kingdom, 45 × 55 mm for Bangladesh, and so on. Every country in the picker links to the official requirement page, because the rules can change and you should be able to check them yourself instead of trusting a website.

Why it stays free

Advertising through Google AdSense pays for the bandwidth, the domain and the models. The ads are deliberately placed out of the way of the work — no pop-ups over the crop tool, no full-screen interstitial before an export, and never inside the download itself. No export carries a watermark, no feature is locked behind a payment, and you do not need an account to make a photo.

Nothing is sold about you. Photos never leave the device, and the only visitor information is the consent-based Firebase ID described in the privacy policy.

Part of something bigger

Passport Room is the first public tool from OneRoom, a project that aims to put a set of genuinely useful tools on the web for free. The OneRoom page explains what the project is trying to do and what comes next.

Send a suggestion

Feature ideas and complaints both help. Write to support.passport.room@gmail.com or use the contact page. Screenshots and the country you were working with make a bug much faster to fix.
