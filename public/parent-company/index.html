OneRoom: the project behind Passport Room

OneRoom is the name our small team works under. The idea is simple and a little stubborn: take the everyday tasks that people usually pay for, get wrong, or hand their files to a stranger to do — and build a free browser tool that does them properly, on your own device.

OneRoom is the parent project. Passport Room is its first public tool, and the rest of the plan is being built on the same rules.

What OneRoom is trying to do

Most free tools on the web are free in name only. They cut the feature you needed, add a watermark over the result, ask you to make an account, or quietly keep the file you uploaded. The paid ones often do the job well, but a passport photo, a signature crop or a document resize is not something most people should have to pay for every few months.

So OneRoom has three rules that every tool in the project has to follow:

  • Free, completely. The core tool is not a trial, has no watermark and does not lock features behind a subscription.
  • Your files stay yours. Wherever the browser can do the work, the work happens in the browser. No upload, no server copy, no “we delete it after 24 hours” promise to take on faith.
  • Finished, not a demo. A tool ships when it covers the real cases people run into, not just the happy path in the marketing screenshot.

Why browsers, and not an app

A web page opens on anything with a browser: an old Android phone, a borrowed laptop, a work computer where you cannot install software. There is nothing to download from a store, nothing to update, and nothing left behind on the machine afterwards. Modern browsers are also good enough to run the heavy parts — Passport Room does background segmentation and face cropping right in the page, and it is fast enough that people use it on phones.

The trade-off is honest: a browser tool needs a network connection the first time so the model files can download. After that, Passport Room keeps working offline.

What exists today

Passport Room is live and free. It removes the background, changes the outfit, enhances the face, lets you set any custom size in millimetres, swaps the background colour, tiles copies onto an A4 or 4 × 6 sheet with a layout editor, and exports a single image or sends the sheet straight to a printer. Sizes for around 195 countries are built in.

It is the first tool, not the whole project. Other everyday jobs around photos and documents are being built on the same foundation — same privacy rules, same no-watermark export, same refusal to put the useful part behind a paywall. We would rather ship one tool that works than ten that almost do.

How it is paid for

The project runs on advertising through Google AdSense. That covers hosting, the domain and the model files. The placements are chosen so they do not interrupt the actual task, and no tool in OneRoom will ever sell your files, your photos or a profile built from them. There is no investor to satisfy, which is exactly why the tools can stay free and stay small.

Who is building it

A small team in Jamalpur, Bangladesh, led by the founder of Passport Room. You can read his message and how the team works on the About CEO page. Suggestions sent to support.passport.room@gmail.com genuinely shape what gets built next — several features in Passport Room started as a message from someone who needed a size that was missing.

Where it is going

Slowly, and in public. Each new OneRoom tool will appear on this site when it is ready to be used, not as a “coming soon” page. If you want to follow along or tell us which tool you need most, the contact page is the place.
