Passport photo sizes by country: the complete guide

Every passport office publishes its own millimetre rules, and a photo that is two millimetres off is the most common reason a counter clerk sends you back to redo it. Here are the sizes for the countries Passport Room ships presets for, in millimetres and inches, with the head-height ratios that matter just as much as the paper size.

A quick sanity check before the table: passport photo size is only half the requirement. The other half is where your head sits inside that rectangle — the head height, measured from the top of your hair (or head, if bare) to the bottom of your chin. A perfectly sized photo with the head floating too high or too low gets rejected just as fast as a wrongly sized one.

Millimetres, inches and DPI: how the numbers connect

A 35 × 45 mm photo printed at 300 DPI is 413 × 531 pixels — that is the resolution a printer needs to produce a crisp physical copy. Digital-only submissions still want a high-resolution file (most offices specify a minimum pixel count or file size), so there is no downside to exporting at full 300 DPI quality even when the photo will never touch paper. When you resize a photo yourself in any editor, resize proportionally and crop instead of stretching: a 35 × 45 mm canvas filled with a photo squashed from a different aspect ratio changes the shape of your face, and clerks notice.

FAQ

Is 35×45 mm the same as passport size?

For most of the world outside North America, yes — the UK, EU, Australia and India all use 35 × 45 mm. The United States uses 2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm) for passports and Canada uses 50 × 70 mm, so the "standard" depends entirely on which document you are applying for.

What size is a US passport photo?

2 × 2 inches — 51 × 51 mm — with the head between 25 mm and 35 mm tall, on a white or off-white background. Digital submissions must be high resolution; the photo maker exports US preset photos at 600 × 600 pixels at 300 DPI.

Can I use the same photo for passport and visa?

Often not without changing the size. A China visa wants 33 × 48 mm while most European documents want 35 × 45 mm. Since the tool re-frames your cut-out into any preset, the same source photo can be rendered at two sizes in two minutes — you just have to export each size separately.
