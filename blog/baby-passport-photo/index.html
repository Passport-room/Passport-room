Baby and toddler passport photos: how to get it right

A baby cannot hold a neutral expression, sit still, or care about your deadline — and yet their passport needs the same millimetre-perfect photo as yours. The good news: every baby passport photo can be taken at home, on a bed or a car seat, with equipment you already own.

Which rules actually relax for babies

Photo standards are written for adults, so passport offices publish extra allowances for children. The exact allowances differ by country, but the pattern is consistent:

  • Expression: infants are not expected to keep a neutral expression or look straight into the lens with the same discipline as adults. Eyes open is usually the hard requirement; a closed-mouth grimace is rarely fatal for a newborn.
  • Head position: very young babies cannot hold their head square to the camera, and offices know it. Small tilts are tolerated for infants in a way they never are for adults.
  • Head covering: hats and headbands that are worn daily for religious or medical reasons follow the adult rules — otherwise, take it off for the photo.

What does not relax: the photo size, the plain background, the "no shadows" rule and — in most countries — a ban on hands and other people in the frame. That last one is where most baby photos fail, and it is solvable with positioning, not editing.

Check your own office first. Each passport authority publishes its own children's photo rules, and they change. This guide describes the common pattern, not a guarantee — the office processing your application is the only judge.

The home setup that works

For a newborn (0–3 months)

Lay a plain white or off-white fitted sheet over a mattress or changing pad so the background is the bed, with no pillows or toys in frame. Photograph from directly above while the baby lies on their back, in daylight from a window — never with a flash, which creates a hard shadow and startles the baby. Support the head from underneath the sheet if needed, so no hand appears in the frame. Shoot burst mode during a calm, wakeful window after a feed, and pick the frame with both eyes open and the face square-ish to the camera.

For a sitting baby (4–12 months)

Sit the baby on a plain surface or in a high chair pulled in front of a plain wall, with a white sheet behind if the wall is patterned. Get down to the baby's eye level — photos taken from above distort the face proportions. Use a toy held next to the lens or a second adult behind the phone to hold attention. Take many frames; you need exactly one good one.

For a toddler (1–3 years)

Toddlers can hold a neutral expression for roughly two seconds, so everything is about timing. Ask them to "show me your funny face, now show me your calm face" and shoot through the whole sequence. Keep the plain-wall background, keep their hands out of frame, and be prepared to accept a mildly unimpressed expression — offices are far more tolerant of a toddler's deadpan stare than of a tilted head.

FAQ

Does a newborn really need a passport photo?

Yes — every passport applicant of any age needs one, and it must meet the same size and background standard as an adult photo. Only some expression and head-position rules are relaxed for infants, and the specifics depend on your country's passport office.

Can a baby smile in a passport photo?

Offices generally do not reject a newborn's natural expression, but the safest interpretation across countries is: eyes open, mouth closed, no exaggerated grin. Toddlers are expected to manage a neutral face, with more lenience than adults.

How fresh does a baby's passport photo need to be?

Baby photos age faster than adult photos — some offices explicitly ask for a recent photo of children precisely because young faces change quickly. Take the photo close to when you submit the application rather than months in advance, and follow your office's own recency rule.
