How to take a passport photo at home that doesn't get rejected

Almost every passport photo rejected at the counter fails for one of five reasons — bad light, busy background, wrong head position, wrong expression, or a low-quality source photo. All five are avoidable at home with a phone, a plain wall and ten minutes of daylight.

The five-minute source photo

Everything that follows assumes one thing: the source photo is decent. No amount of AI editing turns a blurry, dark, angled selfie into a compliant portrait, and a photo of a photo (rephotographing a print) fails quality checks almost everywhere. Get the source right and the rest is mechanical.

1. Light: window daylight, facing the window

Stand about a metre in front of a plain wall, facing a bright window. The window lights your face evenly and leaves no shadow on the wall behind you. Overcast midday light is ideal; direct sun creates hard shadows and makes you squint. Do not use a flash — it flattens features, causes red-eye and throws a hard shadow onto the wall. Avoid ceiling lights as the main source: they cast shadows under the eyes and give skin an odd colour cast that a white-background photo makes obvious.

2. Camera: eye level, arm's length, rear lens

Hold the phone at your eye height, around arm's length or slightly more, using the rear camera (the front camera is lower quality on most phones). A selfie stick or a second person makes this easier. The lens must be level with your eyes — tilted-up phone angles distort the head shape, which is an automatic reject. Have someone else shoot, or use the timer, so both hands are out of frame.

3. Framing: chest up, head and shoulders centred

Include your head and the top of your shoulders, with space above the head — the tool needs pixels to work with when it places your head inside the size guides. Do not pre-crop tightly; crop after the cut-out, when the head-height guides tell you exactly where the frame should sit.

4. Expression and clothing

Neutral expression, mouth closed, both eyes open, looking at the lens. Daily glasses are usually fine if there is no glare and the frames do not hide the eyes; sunglasses are not. Head coverings worn for religious or medical reasons are generally allowed if the full face is visible from chin to crown. Wear what you would wear to an office — dark tops read well against a white background, and uniform-style clothing or camouflage is not permitted in some countries.

5. Background: plain wall wins, AI removal covers the rest

The compliant backgrounds are white, off-white, light grey and (for some countries) light blue. A plain white wall is perfect. A plain wall still gives the cleanest edge, especially around fine hair.

FAQ

Can I take my own passport photo on my phone?

Yes. Passport offices care about the result, not the equipment: a phone photo that is sharp, evenly lit, neutral and correctly sized meets the same standard as a studio shot. The printing guide covers turning that file into physical prints if you need them.

Why do home passport photos get rejected?

The recurring causes are shadows on the background, the head sitting too high or too low in the frame, a tilted camera angle, closed eyes or an open mouth, and photos that are too low resolution. Each has a fix in the checklist above.

Is a white background required?

Most countries want white or off-white; a few accept light grey or light blue. Because the tool replaces the background after the cut-out, you can meet whichever colour your country asks for without re-shooting.

Do I need someone to take the photo for me?

It helps, because a second person keeps the camera at eye level and your hands out of frame. A tripod or timer works too — just check that the lens ends up at your eye height, not looking up at you.
